How this was measured, and its limits
  • Detector. FlashGuard checks every frame against the WCAG 2.x three-flashes rule: a flash is a pair of opposing brightness changes of at least 10%, it must cover at least 25% of a 10° field of view, and more than 3 flashes in any one second fails. Saturated red is checked separately. Camera motion is filtered out by counting the net flashing area.
  • Sample. 40 random games per genre from SteamSpy (Action, RPG, Strategy, Simulation, Sports, Indie), each with at least about 20,000 owners. Only each game's main trailer was scanned, downloaded at low resolution and deleted after scanning. Adventure, Racing and Casual are missing because SteamSpy returned no games for them.
  • Spot check. Still frames from 6 random flagged trailers were reviewed: 4 were clear real flashes and 2 were borderline false alarms, both at exactly 3.5 flashes per second. The "clear fail" line (4+ per second) leaves those borderline cases out, and the finding holds at about 30%.
  • Not a certified test. This is an automated screen, not a Harding test. A flagged trailer has been flagged by the screen, not proven dangerous. Pattern reversals (flipping stripes or checkerboards) aren't detected yet, so the true rate could be higher.
  • Data. The numbers on this page are calculated in your browser from the study's CSV files: games.csv, scans.csv and incidents.csv.
